## Where this application wins

**Requirement 4 is the whole argument.** The posting wants someone who turns messy operational data into reporting that drives real decisions, not just dashboards nobody acts on. The carrier scorecard is exactly that, with a strong, specific proof point: two carrier renegotiations and one termination came directly out of Jordan's analysis. That beats "built dashboards" by a wide margin, and it's the atom to lead with.

**Second:** the freight-billing audit is a near-literal precedent for "root-cause analysis when clinics report billing or scheduling discrepancies" — it's the same shape of work (investigate a discrepancy pattern, trace it to its source, fix the recurrence) in a different industry.

## Where this application is thin

- **Healthcare domain experience** — a clean gap, and it's the single most emphasized nice-to-have in the posting ("strongly preferred"). This is the thing to address head-on in the cover letter rather than hope it goes unnoticed.
- **Clinical-audience presentation** — real stakeholder presentation experience exists, but not with clinical staff specifically, and the person's own review flags presenting to senior audiences as still developing. Don't oversell polish here; the resume should claim the experience, not imply mastery.
- **HIPAA** — low-cost gap, not worth a resume line either way.

This resume leads with the scorecard and the billing audit, in that order, because they map most directly to the posting's top two must-haves.
