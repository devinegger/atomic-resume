# Voice — Jordan Vale

Observed from: the "employee comments" section of the 2025 performance review (the
only material in the inbox that's unmistakably Jordan's own writing) and answers
given during the digest, 2026-02-10.

## How Jordan writes

- Plain, short sentences. Not clipped, just no filler.
- Undercuts a big claim immediately with the real mechanism: *"what made it work
  wasn't really the analysis — it was getting the carrier managers to agree..."*
  Keep this instinct in cover letters — Jordan explains the actual cause, not the
  impressive-sounding one.
- Comfortable naming what almost went wrong: *"the whole thing would have fallen
  apart in the first review."* Don't sand this off into pure confidence — a
  little acknowledged risk reads as more credible, not less.
- Says "I did X" directly. No passive voice, no "was involved in."

## Never

- The 2021 resume's objective-statement register ("detail-oriented operations
  professional seeking to leverage analytical skills in a dynamic, fast-paced
  environment") — flagged during the digest as exactly what this file exists to
  prevent. If a draft starts sounding like that sentence, rewrite it.
- Self-description without a bullet behind it: "results-driven," "detail-oriented."
- Overclaiming precision on the invoice-queue number — Jordan was specific in
  conversation that it was "roughly" and "a team impression," and that hedge is
  accurate, not weak. Keep it.

## For cover letters

Jordan's instinct to explain the real mechanism behind a result (see above) is
the strongest asset for a cover letter specifically — a resume bullet can't hold
that kind of explanation, and a cover letter can. Lead with it rather than with a
summary of the resume.
