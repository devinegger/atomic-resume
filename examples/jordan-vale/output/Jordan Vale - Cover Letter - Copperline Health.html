Dear Care Access Hiring Team,

I'm applying for the Product Analyst role on your Care Access team. The posting's description of the job — turning messy operational data into reporting that clinical staff can actually act on, and running root-cause analysis when something doesn't add up — is close to word-for-word what I've spent the last three years doing in freight logistics, and it's the part of my work I've liked most.

The clearest example: I built a carrier performance scorecard from shipment and claims data that regional leadership now uses in every quarterly review. The hard part wasn't the analysis — it was getting a dozen carrier account managers to agree on a single definition of "on-time" before I measured anything, because without that agreement every number I produced afterward would have been contestable. That groundwork took about a month and nobody would have noticed if I'd skipped it. The scorecard has since driven two carrier renegotiations and one termination, and on-time delivery across the carriers we track moved from 82% to 91%.

The root-cause side of the job I've also done directly, just against a different kind of discrepancy. Freight billing had a pattern of accessorial overcharges that nobody had connected — small enough individually that they read as noise, until I lined up eighteen months of invoices side by side and the pattern became obvious. I worked the specific charges through with each carrier until we agreed on what was owed, recovered $184,000, and then built a recurring check so the same pattern gets caught automatically going forward rather than requiring another manual audit a year from now. That last part matters to me — a one-time recovery is nice, a system that prevents the recurrence is the actual fix.

The honest gap: I don't have healthcare or clinical operations experience. What I bring instead is a track record of getting operational teams — carrier managers, dispatch, warehouse — to trust a new number enough to act on it, and of tracing a messy discrepancy back to a root cause rather than patching the symptom. I think that's the same underlying skill your posting is asking for, applied to a domain I haven't worked in yet, and I'd rather say that plainly than imply otherwise.

What draws me to this role specifically is the shape of the problem: Care Access sits at the point where a scheduling or intake friction point turns into a real cost for a clinic, which is exactly the kind of gap between "the data looks fine" and "something is actually wrong" that I've spent my career closing. I'd expect the ramp-up to include real time learning the clinical and billing context your team already has, and I'm genuinely looking forward to that part rather than treating it as a formality.

I'd welcome the chance to talk about how that experience maps onto what your Care Access team is trying to build. Thanks for considering my application.
