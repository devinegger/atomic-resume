---
template: cover-letter-base
format: base
target_length: 250-325 words, four paragraphs
use_when: Default choice. Most applications.
rules:
  - Every claim must trace to an atom in profile/. No invented enthusiasm, no invented familiarity with the company.
  - Do not restate the resume. Pick two things and give them the context the resume couldn't fit.
  - Write in the user's voice. If their atoms sound plain and direct, the letter sounds plain and direct.
  - Never open with "I am writing to apply for."
  - If the hiring manager's name isn't findable, use the team or department. Never "To Whom It May Concern."

Dear {{NAME or TEAM}},

{{OPENING — two or three sentences. Name the role. Then say the specific thing that made you apply: a problem in the posting you've solved before, something about how the organization works, or the piece of the job that's genuinely the work you want to be doing. Concrete, not flattering.}}

{{EVIDENCE — four to six sentences. Take the single most relevant thing you've done and tell it properly: the situation, what you did, what came of it. This is the paragraph that earns the interview. One story told well beats four mentioned in passing.}}

{{FIT — three or four sentences. Connect that to what this role needs. If there's a real gap between your background and the posting, this is where you address it honestly and say what you'd bring instead. Don't pretend the gap isn't there; don't apologize for it either.}}

{{CLOSE — two sentences. Say what you'd want to talk about in a conversation, and thank them. No pleading, no "I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience."}}

Sincerely,
