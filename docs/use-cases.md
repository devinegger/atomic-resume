# Use cases

The [skills index](../skills/README.md) answers *what does this system do*. This file answers the more common question: **something is going on, what should I actually ask for?**

Each one below is a situation, what's usually going on underneath it, and what to do. Say it in plain language — none of this needs a command, and the agent will tell you if it disagrees with the diagnosis.

---

## "I've been applying to everything and nothing's landing"

The instinct is that the documents are wrong. Usually they aren't. If you've sent fifteen applications and the profile behind them holds twelve atoms, every one of those applications was the same twelve facts in a different order — tailored honestly, and thin in the same place every time.

There's a second possibility worth saying out loud: sometimes it's the market, or the roles are a genuine stretch, and no document fixes that. The fit analysis on each application already told you which. If it kept saying *gap* in the same place, that's a real signal and not a formatting problem.

**Do this:** pick the posting you cared most about and run a [deep dive](../skills/run-a-deep-dive.md) on it. It's the only thing here that makes the profile permanently bigger rather than rearranging what's already in it. Then go back to the ones you'd still take.

**Cost:** a day or more for the dive, depending on the posting. The profile it leaves behind is permanent.

## "This posting is the one"

It landed and your reaction was *I want this job* — and you feel qualified, whether or not your profile currently proves it.

That second half is the signal that matters. Feeling more qualified than your written record makes you look means the gap is in the record, not in you. That's the exact condition a deep dive fixes, and it's why the trigger isn't simply wanting the job.

**Do this:** ask for a [deep dive](../skills/run-a-deep-dive.md) instead of the normal loop.

**Cost:** a day or more, scaling with how much the posting asks for. Wrong call in a week where you have six things to get out — the agent will say so and offer to scope it down.

## "I have an interview"

The trap here is preparing from your profile. What the interviewer is holding is what you **sent**, and those two drift apart on purpose — applications never edit the profile mid-build, so any correction you made afterward never reached the document in their hands.

The other thing people skip is the mock. It's the only part that tests delivery, and delivery is where prep usually fails.

**Do this:** say an interview got scheduled and who it's with. Ask for [interview prep](../skills/prepare-for-interview.md). Do the mock.

**Cost:** roughly half a day, and it splits across sittings comfortably.

## "I've got old resumes but nothing set up yet"

This is the ordinary starting point. Don't tidy anything first — messy is what `_inbox/` is for, and conflicting versions are useful rather than embarrassing. Two resumes that disagree about a date tell the agent exactly what to ask you about.

A performance review is usually the single most valuable thing you can add, because old resumes are good at saying what you did and bad at saying what changed.

**Do this:** put everything in `_inbox/` and say you've done it.

**Cost:** twenty to forty minutes for the first pass. Applications take a few minutes each after that.

---

## "I've never really written any of this down"

First job, career change, a long stretch of self-employment, or a role where nobody kept records. There's nothing to digest, and starting from an empty `_inbox/` feels like the system isn't for you.

It is. The material exists, it just isn't in a document yet.

**Do this:** say you have nothing to start from and ask for help [gathering source material](../skills/gather-source-material.md). It works by asking rather than by reading.

---

## "I've lost track of what I've applied to"

Past six or so applications, *"which of these have I not heard back from"* stops being answerable by opening folders one at a time. The per-application logs are the right shape for history and the wrong shape for state.

**Do this:** ask for a [tracker](../skills/build-a-tracker.md).

**One thing it will tell you too:** a tracker doesn't update itself, and when it lapses it doesn't go blank — it goes quietly wrong while still looking authoritative. The logs in each folder stay the real record.

## "I just finished something worth recording"

A project shipped, a number came in, or you finally learned what happened to that migration you ran last year. This is the moment the system gets smarter rather than just busier, and it's the easiest one to skip.

**Do this:** just say it. It'll ask a couple of questions and write it down properly — see [maintain-profile](../skills/maintain-profile.md).

**One catch:** mid-application, a new fact goes into your profile *afterward* and is available for the next one. That's deliberate — it's the rule that stops a convenient claim from appearing under deadline pressure.

---

## "They want a format the templates don't cover"

A portal wants a Word file, a recruiter asked for their own template, or you have a design you like.

**Do this:** ask to [adopt the format](../skills/adopt-template-format.md). You'll get an honest warning along with it: heavily designed resumes are the ones applicant tracking systems parse worst, and that trade is yours to make knowingly. [`ats-notes.md`](ats-notes.md) has the detail.

---

## "It won't write down what I tell it"

If it's refusing material you volunteered, or interrogating you about numbers you don't have, that isn't how it's meant to behave. It runs in a forgiving mode by default and something has gone wrong — or it's been switched into strict mode, which some people turn on deliberately after an interview goes badly on a detail.

**Do this:** tell it. See [`HELP.md`](../HELP.md) → *make it stop being so fussy*.

**What won't move:** whether a claim is supported by your own evidence. That one isn't fussiness — it's the reason the whole thing exists.

---

## "I'm coming back after a few months away"

Everything is still here and nothing expired — these are plain files and nothing is generated on the fly. What's usually stale is the profile, not the system.

**Do this:** say what you've been doing since. That's a [maintain-profile](../skills/maintain-profile.md) conversation, and it's worth having before the next application rather than after it. If you have an interview coming up on something you sent months ago, say that too — the gap between what you sent and what's true now is exactly what [interview prep](../skills/prepare-for-interview.md) sweeps for.
